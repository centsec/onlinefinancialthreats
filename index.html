Online Financial Threats

A catalogue of the online financial threats facing UK adolescents aged 11 to 16.

Placeholder introduction — replace before publication

Adolescents encounter commercial pressure, persuasive design and outright fraud across the games, marketplaces, social platforms and payment services they use every day. These threats are usually studied in separate literatures: dark patterns in one, gambling-like game mechanics in another, online fraud in a third. This taxonomy brings them together into a single catalogue so that they can be compared on equal terms.

Each threat is classified along four axes. Mechanism records how the threat operates on the young person — whether it misleads, dissociates the spend from its cost, activates impulse, applies social influence, introduces chance, or attacks the device or account directly. Domain records where it operates, from games and virtual currency to payments and account security. Legal status records its standing under UK law, distinguishing lawful commercial practice from regulatory breach and from criminal offence. TTM stage records how ready adolescents are to defend against it, using the stages of the Transtheoretical Model, alongside whether that judgement rests on focus-group evidence or on inference from the wider literature.

The classification is descriptive rather than evaluative. A threat recorded as lawful practice is not thereby harmless, and one recorded as a criminal offence is not necessarily the most commonly encountered. Each entry carries the source it was drawn from, so that the basis of every classification can be checked.
